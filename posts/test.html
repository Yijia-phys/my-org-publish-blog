Mathematical environments

Definition 1 — Upper bound

Let \(A \subseteq \mathbb{R}\). A number \(M \in \mathbb{R}\) is an upper bound of \(A\) if \(x \leq M\) for every \(x \in A\).

Axiom 2 — Completeness

Every nonempty subset of \(\mathbb{R}\) that is bounded above has a supremum in \(\mathbb{R}\).

Lemma 3 — Approximation from below

If \(s = \sup A\), then for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there exists \(a \in A\) such that

\[ s - \varepsilon < a \leq s. \]

Theorem 4 — Uniqueness of the supremum

A nonempty subset of \(\mathbb{R}\) has at most one supremum.

Proof.

Suppose \(s\) and \(t\) are both suprema of \(A\). Since \(t\) is an upper bound and \(s\) is the least upper bound, \(s \leq t\). Similarly, \(t \leq s\). Therefore \(s = t\).

□
Corollary 5

If \(A\) has a maximum \(m\), then \(\sup A = m\).

Example 6 — A supremum without a maximum

Let \(A = (0,1)\). Then \(\sup A = 1\), but \(A\) has no maximum because \(1 \notin A\).

For every \(x \in A\), the number \((x+1)/2\) also belongs to \(A\) and is strictly greater than \(x\).
