Let us begin with the definition of the one-sided Laplace transform.

2. Applications

2.1. Transforming a Parameter and Inverting

The first method is to regard an integral as a function of a parameter, take its Laplace transform, and then recover the original function by identifying the inverse transform.

Theorem 5 — Uniqueness and inverse transformation

Let f,g:[0,∞)→Cf,g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}f,g:[0,∞)→C be continuous functions of exponential order. If their Laplace transforms agree for all sufficiently large real sss, then f(x)=g(x)f(x)=g(x)f(x)=g(x) for every x≥0x\geq0x≥0.

Consequently, if F=L{f}F=\mathcal{L}\{f\}F=L{f}, its inverse Laplace transform within this class is uniquely determined and satisfies

L−1{F}(x)=L−1{L{f}}(x)=f(x),x≥0. \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F\}(x) =\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{\mathcal{L}\{f\}\}(x) =f(x),\qquad x\geq0. L−1{F}(x)=L−1{L{f}}(x)=f(x),x≥0.

In practice, we often recover the function by matching its transform with an entry in the table, without using an explicit inversion formula. The theorem guarantees that a matching transform gives the original function.

2.2. Transferring a Transform Between Factors

The following identity lets us move a Laplace transform from one factor of an integral to the other.

Theorem 7 — An integral identity for Laplace transforms

Let F=L{f}F=\mathcal{L}\{f\}F=L{f} and G=L{g}G=\mathcal{L}\{g\}G=L{g}, where fff and ggg are measurable and their transforms converge absolutely for every positive real argument. Suppose also that

∫0∞∫0∞e−st∣f(t)g(s)∣ ds dt<∞. \int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty e^{-st}|f(t)g(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t \lt \infty. ∫0∞​∫0∞​e−st∣f(t)g(s)∣dsdt<∞.

∫0∞f(t)G(t) dt=∫0∞F(s)g(s) ds. \int_0^\infty f(t)G(t)\,\mathrm{d}t =\int_0^\infty F(s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s. ∫0∞​f(t)G(t)dt=∫0∞​F(s)g(s)ds.

Proof.

The absolute integrability assumption allows us to interchange the order of integration:

∫0∞f(t)G(t) dt=∫0∞∫0∞f(t)e−tsg(s) ds dt=∫0∞g(s)(∫0∞e−stf(t) dt)ds=∫0∞F(s)g(s) ds.\begin{aligned} \int_0^\infty f(t)G(t)\,\mathrm{d}t &=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty f(t)e^{-ts}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t \\ &=\int_0^\infty g(s)\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-st}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathrm{d}s \\ &=\int_0^\infty F(s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \end{aligned}∫0∞​f(t)G(t)dt​=∫0∞​∫0∞​f(t)e−tsg(s)dsdt=∫0∞​g(s)(∫0∞​e−stf(t)dt)ds=∫0∞​F(s)g(s)ds.​

□

The integration variables are dummy variables, so we can equally write this as

∫0∞f(x)G(x) dx=∫0∞F(x)g(x) dx. \int_0^\infty f(x)G(x)\,\mathrm{d}x =\int_0^\infty F(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x. ∫0∞​f(x)G(x)dx=∫0∞​F(x)g(x)dx.
