Dedekind's construction

Imagine cutting the number line at a point and keeping everything strictly to its left. Dedekind's idea is to describe the position of the cut using only rational numbers: we record which elements of Q\mathbb{Q}Q lie to its left. A cut can occur at a rational number or at a gap in Q\mathbb{Q}Q, such as the position we wish to call 2\sqrt{2}2​. In this way, we can construct real numbers from sets of rational numbers without assuming that the real numbers already exist.

Definition 1 — Dedekind left set

A Dedekind left set is a subset L⊆QL \subseteq \mathbb{Q}L⊆Q satisfying the following three properties:

  1. Nonempty and proper. There is a rational number in LLL and a rational number outside LLL, i.e. (∃a∈Q) (a∈L)and(∃b∈Q) (b∉L). (\exists a \in \mathbb{Q})\,(a \in L) \quad\text{and}\quad (\exists b \in \mathbb{Q})\,(b \notin L). (∃a∈Q)(a∈L)and(∃b∈Q)(b∈/L).
  2. Downward closed. Every rational number smaller than an element of LLL also belongs to LLL, i.e. (∀p,q∈Q) ((q∈L∧p<q)  ⟹  p∈L). (\forall p,q \in \mathbb{Q})\, \bigl((q \in L \land p<q)\implies p \in L\bigr). (∀p,q∈Q)((q∈L∧p<q)⟹p∈L).
  3. No greatest element. Every element of LLL has a strictly larger element in LLL, i.e. (∀q∈L)(∃r∈L) (q<r). (\forall q \in L)(\exists r \in L)\,(q<r). (∀q∈L)(∃r∈L)(q<r).

For a rational cut point aaa, we take {q∈Q:q<a}\{q \in \mathbb{Q}:q<a\}{q∈Q:q<a}. Replacing q<aq<aq<a by q≤aq\leq aq≤a would include aaa as the greatest element, violating the third property. Requiring no greatest element gives us a consistent convention at rational cut points.
