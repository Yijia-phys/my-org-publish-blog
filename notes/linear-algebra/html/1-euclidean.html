Vectors

We describe a vector by an ordered list of real numbers. Throughout this chapter, nnn is a positive integer.

Dot product

The dot product combines two vectors into a real number. It also gives a way to measure length.

Definition 6 — Dot product and norm

Definition 12 — Orthogonal vectors

Vectors x,y∈Rnx,y\in\mathbb{R}^nx,y∈Rn are orthogonal if x⋅y=0x\cdot y=0x⋅y=0. We write x⊥yx\perp yx⊥y.

Remark 13 — Geometric meaning

In R2\mathbb{R}^2R2 and R3\mathbb{R}^3R3, we picture a vector as an arrow from the origin. Its norm is its length. For two nonzero vectors, orthogonality means that the arrows meet at a right angle. The vectors in Example 8 give an example.

The zero vector is orthogonal to every vector by definition, although it has no direction. For orthogonal vectors, Theorem 9 is the familiar relation between the squared lengths of the sides of a right triangle.
