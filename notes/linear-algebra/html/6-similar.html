Definition 1 — Change-of-basis matrix

Let A=(v1,…,vn)A=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)A=(v1​,…,vn​) and B=(w1,…,wn)B=(w_1,\ldots,w_n)B=(w1​,…,wn​) be ordered bases of a vector space VVV over KKK. Remark 2 — The standard basis

For KnK^nKn, we denote the standard basis by E=(e1,…,en)\mathcal{E}=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)E=(e1​,…,en​). Then (x)E(x)_{\mathcal{E}}(x)E​ is the vector with its usual coordinate column xxx.

To construct CE←AC_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow A}CE←A​, place the basis vectors of AAA into columns, using their standard coordinates. It is an nnn by nnn matrix, with one column for each vector of AAA.

Remark 3 — Bases are ordered here

Throughout this chapter, a basis comes with a specified order. The first coordinate multiplies the first basis vector, and so on. Reordering a basis changes the corresponding coordinate columns and change-of-basis matrices.

Proposition 7 — Coordinate conversion and the identity matrix
  1. For bases A,BA,BA,B of KnK^nKn, the equality (a)A=(b)B=(x)E (a)_A=(b)_B=(x)_{\mathcal{E}} (a)A​=(b)B​=(x)E​ is equivalent to the coordinate relations b=CB←Aa,x=CE←Aa=CE←Bb. b=C_{B\leftarrow A}a,\qquad x=C_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow A}a=C_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow B}b. b=CB←A​a,x=CE←A​a=CE←B​b.   2. For every ordered basis AAA, CA←A=In. C_{A\leftarrow A}=I_n. CA←A​=In​.
Proof.

For the first assertion, each coordinate relation is the defining conversion for the same vector. Conversely, these relations show that all three expressions represent that vector.

The columns of CE←BC_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow B}CE←B​ form a basis, so this matrix is invertible. From CE←Bb=CE←Aa, C_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow B}b=C_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow A}a, CE←B​b=CE←A​a, we obtain b=(CE←B)−1CE←Aab=(C_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow B})^{-1}C_{\mathcal{E}\leftarrow A}ab=(CE←B​)−1CE←A​a. Since this holds for every coordinate column aaa, it gives the displayed matrix formula.

For the second assertion, converting from a basis to itself leaves every coordinate column unchanged. Equivalently, the coordinate column of vjv_jvj​ in its own basis is eje_jej​, so the columns of CA←AC_{A\leftarrow A}CA←A​ are those of InI_nIn​.
