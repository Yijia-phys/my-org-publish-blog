Arithmetic axioms

We begin with the rules governing addition and multiplication of real numbers. These are the field axioms. An abelian group is a set with an operation satisfying closure, associativity, commutativity, an identity law, and an inverse law. Here addition makes R\mathbb{R}R an abelian group, while multiplication makes the nonzero real numbers an abelian group. Distributivity connects the two operations.

Order axioms

The field axioms describe arithmetic. To compare real numbers, we also require an order compatible with addition and multiplication. We write x≤yx\leq yx≤y to mean x<yx<yx<y or x=yx=yx=y.

Axiom 3 — Order axioms for the real numbers

The relation <<< on R\mathbb{R}R satisfies the following properties for all x,y,z∈Rx,y,z\in\mathbb{R}x,y,z∈R.

  1. Trichotomy. Exactly one of x<yx<yx<y, x=yx=yx=y, and y<xy<xy<x holds.
  2. Transitivity. If x<yx<yx<y and y<zy<zy<z, then x<zx<zx<z.
  3. Compatibility with addition. If x<yx<yx<y, then x+z<y+zx+z<y+zx+z<y+z.
  4. Compatibility with multiplication. If x<yx<yx<y and z>0z>0z>0, then xz<yzxz<yzxz<yz.
Remark 4 — Order is an additional structure

A field equipped with an order satisfying these axioms is called an ordered field. The order axioms are additional requirements; they do not follow from the field axioms alone. For example, C\mathbb{C}C is a field, but no order on it can satisfy all these compatibility properties. We will see why from the fact that squares in an ordered field are nonnegative.

Boundedness

The arithmetic and order axioms also hold in Q\mathbb{Q}Q. To distinguish the real numbers, we need a further axiom expressing that there are no gaps. We formulate it using bounds and least upper bounds.

Definition 10 — Upper bounds, lower bounds, and boundedness
